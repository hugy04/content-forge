# ONDC Will Erase the Platform Tax for 12 Million Small Retailers

![A flat vector illustration of a spice jar, a wallet, and a textile arranged on a grid and connected by lines to a central hub in green and clay tones.](image.png)

In the 18 months since its initial pilot, ONDC has demonstrated that decoupling digital discovery from delivery can slash retailer commissions from 30% to under 5%.

## What Changed: The Migration from Walled Gardens to Open Protocols

The transition from closed marketplaces like Amazon or Flipkart to the Open Network for Digital Commerce (ONDC) represents a fundamental shift from platform-centric models to network-centric protocols. In a traditional walled garden, a single entity controls the buyer, the seller, and the logistics. ONDC unbundles these layers. Sellers no longer need to maintain separate, redundant inventories across multiple apps to reach a broad customer base. Instead, a single catalog upload to the network makes a kirana store visible to any buyer-side application—whether it is a banking app like Pincode or a specialized discovery interface. This interoperability ensures that consumer choice is no longer dictated by which app they have installed, but by product availability and proximity.

## Drivers: Why the Marketplace Model is Structurally Inefficient

The legacy marketplace model is buckling under its own weight. High Customer Acquisition Costs (CAC) for individual platforms are currently unsustainable, forcing them to extract high commissions—often between 25% and 30%—from small retailers to subsidize marketing and overhead. Monopolistic control over logistics and payments has historically prevented small businesses from choosing more cost-effective third-party providers. ONDC mirrors the success of UPI in payments by providing a regulatory framework for decentralized infrastructure. This government-led mandate for interoperability is essential for penetrating Tier-2 and Tier-3 markets, where the thin margins of local retailers cannot survive the predatory "platform tax" required by urban-centric e-commerce giants.

## Evidence: Initial Scale Proves the Interoperability Thesis

Early pilots in cities like Bangalore and Kochi show a significant increase in non-food categories participating in the network, ranging from electronics to home decor. Major FMCG players, including Hindustan Unilever and ITC, are already bypassing traditional multi-tiered distribution by linking their distributor networks directly to the ONDC grid. This allows for hyper-local fulfillment from the nearest kirana store rather than a distant warehouse. Data from current transactions indicates a 60% reduction in end-to-end transaction costs for merchants who leverage independent, hyper-local delivery services through the network. The ability to choose a delivery partner based on real-time performance rather than platform mandate is already shifting power back to the merchant.

## Second-Order Effects: How Data Sovereignty Redefines Small Business Credit

The most significant missed impact is the emergence of 'Flow-Based Lending.' Under ONDC, every transaction creates a verifiable, immutable digital trail that acts as collateral. Retailers previously deemed "unbankable" can now access unsecured loans based on real-time sales velocity. Furthermore, hyper-local logistics will become a standardized utility, causing specialized delivery startups to compete purely on reliability and speed rather than platform-exclusive rights. 

Digital marketing will also shift from paying for placement on a marketplace to paying for visibility on 'discovery-only' apps that do not take a cut of the sale. This restores price-setting power to the retailer, as they are no longer subject to algorithmic suppression by platform-owned private labels. Finally, inventory management software will transition from a backend record-keeping tool to a real-time broadcast engine. When a shopkeeper scans a shipment into their POS system, that inventory is instantly live across the entire network, making every physical shelf a node in a decentralized national warehouse.
